R1.1 (methodological novelty vs. Sasaki et al. and established components)
----------------------------------------------------------------------

contributions as follows: (i) a formulation of mask-free gap closing whose
objective is fillability, which implies that the input ink must be preserved

guarantee topological closure. We added a topological criterion [EXP-6]: a gap
is considered closed if the two ground-truth regions on each side of it are not

(vs. 70.5% with the pixel criterion). We keep the pixel criterion for comparison

R1.7 (dataset generation protocol, deduplication, seeds)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The revised dataset section documents the generation protocol. Skeletons are
obtained with the scikit-image skeletonization of the ink (gray level < 180),
after pruning spurs shorter than about the stroke width. Junction stubs: at each
image, a fraction drawn uniformly from [0.5, 1.0] of the eligible T-junctions
is cut; the visible stub length is drawn uniformly from 2-20 pixels, at most 50%
of the arriving branch is removed, and branches shorter than 20 pixels are not
cut. Mid-stroke gaps: 4-12 gaps per image (uniform), with length uniform in
4-60 pixels; the branch is chosen with probability proportional to its length
and the position uniformly along it; gaps keep a margin of 8 pixels from
endpoints, junctions and other gaps; branches shorter than 30 pixels are not cut;
at most 35% of a branch and 25% of a connected shape are removed. Shapes whose
skeleton is shorter than 120 pixels are never cut. Erasure removes the full
stroke width, including the anti-aliased fringe. The train/test split and all
corruptions are seeded (split seed 1234; per-image seeds derived from it for the
test set and for each training variant), and the split manifest with all
parameters is distributed with the dataset. Regarding deduplication, the split
is made at the level of source images; we additionally checked for near
duplicates across the split with perceptual hashing and found [XX] [EXP-8].

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R2.7 (loss functions and notation)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
We now give the loss as equations. The network outputs a background logit z;
the background probability is sigmoid(z) and the ink probability is
p = sigmoid(-z) = 1 - sigmoid(z). The weighted cross-entropy is computed on the
background probability against the clean target, with weight 3 on hole pixels
and 1 elsewhere, averaged over all pixels (weights are not renormalized). Both
Dice terms are computed on the ink probability p against the binarized clean ink
(target gray level < 0.5), per image with smoothing constant 1, and averaged over
the batch. The hole mask is the set of pixels that are ink in the target and
white (> 0.9) in the input. The local Dice term multiplies p and the target by a
neighborhood mask obtained by max-pooling the hole mask with a 15x15 window, i.e.
radius r = 7 around each erased pixel, which is the "15-pixel-wide neighborhood"
of the text (now stated consistently). For samples without gaps, the local term
equals zero because of the smoothing constant. Total loss: L = L_BCE + L_Dice +
0.5 L_localDice.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.5 (diffusion-based or blind inpainting baselines)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Generative and diffusion-based inpainting models require the mask of the missing
region, which is exactly what is unknown in our task, and blind inpainting models
such as VCNet target natural photographs, where corruption is a foreign pattern
rather than missing ink indistinguishable from the background (as discussed in
the related work). Moreover, generative models regenerate image content, and our
results show that any change to intact strokes creates new gaps. For these
reasons, we consider the mask-free line-art methods, the input-preserving
baseline and the segmentation-level methods [EXP-2, EXP-5] the most relevant
comparators. We clarified this choice in the text.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.6 (resolution and aspect ratio)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
We agree that production images vary in size. The convolutional part of the
network is resolution independent, but the transformer blocks use positional
embeddings for fixed 28x28 and 56x56 grids, so images of other sizes are
processed by padding to a square and resizing, or, for large canvases, by
overlapping 896x896 tiles. [Optional EXP-9: we tested tiled inference on test
drawings upscaled to 1792x1792 and obtained XX.] We added this discussion to the
limitations; training at several resolutions is part of our future work.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

----------------------------------------------------------------------
We would like to clarify that the drawings are not reduced to one-pixel lines.

We also added an analysis of gap closure and bucket accuracy as a function of

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.9 (failures on long curved contours)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The receptive field is not the limiting factor in these cases: the 28x28
bottleneck attention gives every position access to the whole image. These
failures occur when a long part of a curved contour is missing and its shape
cannot be inferred from its two ends (for instance the lobe of a leaf), so the
problem is ambiguous rather than limited by the architecture; in these cases the
network leaves the gap open rather than guessing. We added this analysis to the
text and mention, as possible remedies, shape priors learned from larger and
more specific datasets, or user hints for such long gaps.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

----------------------------------------------------------------------

a laptop CPU [EXP-11, optional]. We added a sentence on possible optimizations

R3.13 (complexity of transformer blocks vs. lighter alternatives)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The new ablation [EXP-1] compares the full model with lighter variants (without
