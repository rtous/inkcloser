RESPONSE TO REVIEWERS
Manuscript MM-2026-10-0109, "InkCloser: Line-Art Completion for Paint-Bucket Colorization"

======================================================================
GENERAL RESPONSE TO THE EDITOR
======================================================================

We thank the Editor and the three reviewers for their careful and constructive
reading of our manuscript. The main changes are:

1. Ablation study. A new section and table (Table 2) evaluate six variants of
   InkCloser, from a plain U-Net trained with cross-entropy to variants without
   each context module, without gated convolutions and without the local Dice
   term.
2. Metrics. Region matching, leaks and the gap-closure criterion are defined
   precisely, and their scope is stated more carefully.
3. Method and data. The loss, training settings and corruption protocol are
   described more completely, and the contributions state the difference with
   respect to Sasaki et al. more clearly.
4. Claims. Statements about the attention modules and about the baselines have
   been adjusted to the results.

Changes are marked in blue in the annotated version of the manuscript.

REVIEWER 1
======================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R1.1 (methodological novelty vs. Sasaki et al. and established components)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The contributions paragraph of the introduction now states the main difference
with respect to Sasaki et al.: our network preserves the intact strokes, whereas
theirs regenerates the whole drawing, which creates new gaps (it keeps 94.1% of
the input ink). The new ablation study provides experimental support for the
individual components (see R1.2).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R1.2 (component ablations)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
We added the requested ablation study (new section "Ablation study" and
Table 2). It covers the four variants requested (without the 28x28 attention,
without the 56x56 branch, with standard instead of gated convolutions, and
without the local Dice term) and a plain U-Net trained with our loss and with
cross-entropy only, reporting gap closure, bucket accuracy, leaks per image,
fill F1, leak-free drawings and parameters, with 95% bootstrap confidence
intervals. To keep the cost manageable, variants are trained for one epoch,
except the comparison of the bottleneck attention, which needs the full training
length and is run at it. In short: the local Dice term has the largest effect
(-11.2 points of gap closure without it); gated convolutions mainly improve the
accuracy of the added ink (-7.1 points of fill F1 without them); the 56x56 branch
helps close gaps (-3.4 points); the bottleneck attention slightly improves fill
F1 and bucket accuracy but does not help close gaps (+3.6 points without it),
which we report as such; and the full architecture improves every metric of a
plain U-Net trained with the same loss. We also revised the two sentences that
attributed the improvement, and the closure of long gaps, to the attention
modules, so that they agree with these results.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R1.3 (input preservation as alternative explanation)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The ablation study addresses this question with input-preserving models
trained under the same budget. The plain U-Net, whose skip connections also let
it copy the input, is the simple input-preserving completion model suggested by
the reviewer. Trained with our loss, it closes 27.3% of the gaps, against 42.2%
for InkCloser under the same budget, and its bucket accuracy is 84.8% against
90.0%. Input preservation therefore explains part of the gain over Sasaki et
al., and the architecture and loss account for a substantial further part.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R1.4 (synthetic gaps; naturally damaged artwork)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
We agree that this is the main limitation of the study, and the "Lessons and
limitations" section states it explicitly: real gaps caused by drawing,
scanning, compression or line extraction may differ from our synthetic ones,
and validating the approach on naturally occurring gaps and on other line
styles, resolutions and stroke widths is the next step of this work. Building a
benchmark of real damaged artwork with reliable leak annotations is beyond the
scope of this revision.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R1.5 (trapped-ball, Fourey et al., Delaunay Painting as comparators)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Trapped-ball segmentation, the method of Fourey et al. and Delaunay Painting
make the fill robust to gaps at coloring time: they produce a segmentation or a
coloring, and Delaunay Painting and LazyBrush require user color hints. They do
not restore the line art, so their output cannot be evaluated with the same
metrics under the same assumptions (single automatic pass, no user input). As
stated in the introduction, InkCloser is complementary to them: its restored
line art is closed for any tool, including these. The statement about the
baselines not improving on the input is now restricted to bucket accuracy (see
R2, minor comment 7).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R1.6 (80% gap-closure criterion and IoU-based bucket accuracy)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
We agree that pixel coverage does not guarantee topological closure, and the
text now says so explicitly, noting that the two can disagree in both
directions. For this reason, the paint-bucket metrics, which are topological,
are now presented as the primary measure of success. Regarding bucket accuracy,
the regions are not an approximation of flood filling: each region is a
4-connected background area, exactly what a paint-bucket click without gap
tolerance fills from any seed inside it. The IoU only decides whether that fill
matches the original region. The text now states this, together with the
precise matching and leak rules (see R2.3). A per-gap topological criterion is
an interesting addition that we leave for future work.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R1.7 (dataset generation protocol, deduplication, seeds)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The dataset section now states the distribution of gap lengths (uniform
between 4 and 60 pixels), that the skeleton only determines where ink is removed
(each cut erases the full stroke width), and that the generation code, with all
its parameters and random seeds, is released with the dataset. The released
generator documents the remaining details (skeleton extraction and pruning,
stub lengths, margins, eligibility and protection thresholds). The train/test
split is made at the level of source images and is seeded; we did not perform an
additional near-duplicate check across source images.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R1.8 (training settings, convergence, variants seen, loss weights)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The training section now reports the full settings: AdamW with learning rate
1e-3 (3e-4 for the transformer blocks), weight decay 1e-4, cosine learning-rate
schedule, gradient clipping at norm 1.0, mixed precision and batch size 4. There
is no checkpoint selection: we use the model after the last training step.
Regarding the corrupted variants, each pass over the data draws one of the six
variants of every drawing at random, so the network sees several different
variants of each drawing, combined with random geometric augmentation that makes
every sample different. The loss weights were chosen in preliminary experiments;
the text now says so, and the ablation study quantifies the effect of the local
Dice term. Variability across training runs is discussed in the ablation study:
two runs of the same configuration differ noticeably in gap closure, and we
state that measuring this variance would require several runs per configuration.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R1.9 (confidence intervals, bootstrap details, Figure 4 bin counts)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The text now states the number of bootstrap resamples (1,000, paired across
methods). All the improvements of InkCloser over every baseline in Table 1 are
significant at p <= 0.001, the smallest value attainable with 1,000 resamples.
The ablation table reports 95% confidence intervals for every value. Regarding
Figure 4, the caption already notes that the last bin contains only 15 gaps, and
we agree that it should not be read with the same confidence as the other bins.
Variability across training runs is discussed in the ablation study (see R1.8).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R1.10 (random failures, merged regions, extended intentional strokes)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The "Visual quality and failures" paragraph describes the main failure modes:
long missing stretches of curved contours whose shape cannot be inferred from
their ends, and areas with many close, nearly parallel strokes. Since InkCloser
keeps 99.9998% of the input ink, it practically never opens new gaps; the
remaining topological errors are unrepaired gaps and, less often, added ink that
closes a passage. Given the length limits, a figure of randomly selected
failures and a separate count of each error type are left for future work.

R2.2 (gap closure is not topological closure)

We agree. The text now states that the 80% criterion measures redrawn ink

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R2.3 (definitions of region and leak metrics; splits and disappearances)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The metrics are now specified as implemented: regions are 4-connected
background components (paired with 8-connected ink), the exterior background is
included, and regions under 20 pixels are ignored; each ground-truth region is
matched to the output region with the highest IoU (many-to-one matching is
allowed, although IoU >= 0.9 makes it effectively one-to-one); bucket accuracy is
pooled over all regions of all test images. "Most" is replaced by an explicit
threshold: a leak occurs when an output region contains at least 50% of each of
two ground-truth regions, and an output region that absorbs k regions counts as
k-1 leaks. We agree that leak-free does not mean "no correction needed" and
removed that equivalence; the text now notes that a leak-free drawing may still
contain split regions or spurious strokes. Split and lost regions are already
penalized by bucket accuracy, since such a region fails the IoU >= 0.9 match;
given the length limits, we did not add separate statistics for them.

R2.4 (ablations)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Please see our answer to R1.2. The new Table 2 includes the configurations
suggested by the reviewer: a standard U-Net with a basic loss (cross-entropy), a
standard U-Net with the proposed loss, and variants without gated convolutions,
without each attention branch and without the local Dice term, with region-level
metrics and parameter counts.

R2.5 (baselines; suggested references)

R2.6 (real gaps and negative cases)

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R2.7 (loss functions and notation)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The text now clarifies the main points, and the full definition is as follows.
The network outputs a background logit z; the background probability is
sigmoid(z) and the ink probability is p = 1 - sigmoid(z). The hole mask contains
the pixels that are ink in the target and background (> 0.9) in the input. The
weighted cross-entropy is computed on the background probability against the
clean target, with weight 3 on hole pixels and 1 elsewhere, averaged over all
pixels (weights are not renormalized). Both Dice terms are computed on the ink
probability p against the binarized target ink (gray level < 0.5), per image with
smoothing constant 1, and averaged over the batch. The local Dice term multiplies
p and the target by a neighborhood mask obtained by max-pooling the hole mask with
a 15x15 window, i.e. all pixels within r = 7 pixels of an erased pixel; the text
now uses this description, consistent with Figure 2. For samples without gaps the
local term is zero. Total loss: L = L_BCE + L_Dice + 0.5 L_localDice. Given the
length limits, the equations are not included in the article.

R3.1 (real-world gap distributions)

Please see R1.4. The limitation is stated explicitly in the article, and

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.2 (ablation study)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Please see our answer to R1.2 and the new Table 2, which isolates the gated

attention) and the local Dice term.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.3 (low closure of gaps shorter than 8 pixels)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The text explains that this is largely an effect of the closure criterion: for
a gap of a few pixels, a completion that is one pixel thinner or offset fails to
cover 80% of it, whereas the same error is negligible for a long gap. The
remaining short-gap failures are mostly short junction stubs, where the network
must decide whether a stroke ends on purpose just short of another line. As a
remedy, the limitations section proposes weighting the loss towards gaps that
actually merge regions, which directly targets the short gaps that cause leaks.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.4 (only five epochs; convergence)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The training section now reports the full settings, and states that the model
after the last training step is used, without checkpoint selection (see R1.8).
Each pass over the data contains about 2,250 optimizer steps at 896x896 pixels,
with random corrupted variants and augmentation. The ablation study shows that
the structural metrics benefit considerably from longer training (gap closure
42.2% after one epoch vs. 70.5% for the reported model). Further training could
improve the results further, which would only strengthen the conclusions of the
comparison with the baselines.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.5 (diffusion-based or blind inpainting baselines)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Generative and diffusion-based inpainting models require the mask of the
missing region, which is exactly what is unknown here, and blind inpainting
methods such as VCNet target photographs, where the damage differs in appearance
from the image content, whereas a gap in line art looks exactly like background.
Moreover, generative models regenerate image content, and our results show that
any change to intact strokes creates new gaps (see the retrained Sasaki et al.
network, which keeps only 94.1% of the input ink). We added a sentence to the
evaluation section explaining why these models are not included.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.6 (resolution and aspect ratio)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The convolutional parts of the network are independent of resolution, while the
attention modules are configured for fixed grid sizes, so drawings of other
sizes or aspect ratios can be processed by padding and resizing or, for large
canvases, by overlapping tiles. The limitations section states that all results
were obtained at a single resolution, and evaluating other resolutions is part
of the future work stated there.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.7 (stroke thickness)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The drawings are not reduced to one-pixel lines: the skeleton is used only to
decide where to cut, each cut removes the full width of the stroke, and both the
input and the target keep the original stroke widths of the source drawings. The
dataset section now says so. Evaluation on other stroke weights and brush
textures is part of the future work stated in the limitations.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.8 (local Dice weight of 0.5)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The weight was chosen in preliminary experiments, and the text now says so
without claiming that it is optimal. The new ablation study quantifies the
effect of the term: removing it reduces gap closure by 11.2 points and bucket
accuracy by 5.0 points, the largest effect of any component.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.9 (failures on long curved contours)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
These failures occur when a long part of a curved contour is missing and its
shape cannot be inferred from its two ends (for instance, the lobe of a leaf),
as stated in the "Visual quality and failures" paragraph. The problem is
ambiguous rather than limited by the receptive field, since the bottleneck
attention gives every position access to the whole image; in these cases the
network leaves the gap open rather than guessing, which is preferable for
coloring. Shape priors learned from larger, domain-specific data, or user hints
for such gaps, are possible remedies.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.10 (inference time)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
InkCloser is meant as a preprocessing step that runs once per drawing before
coloring, not at every brush stroke, so it does not need real-time rates; 92 ms
on a T4 GPU is imperceptible in that workflow, as stated in the "Speed"
paragraph. Further speed-ups are possible with standard deployment tools (e.g.
ONNX/TensorRT export and batching), which we leave for future work.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.11 (peak memory)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
A peak of 1.24 GB fits in the GPU memory of current artist workstations and
most laptops. Further reductions are possible with integer quantization and
memory-efficient attention, which we leave for future work.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.12 (parallel processing)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
All operations of the network (convolutions and attention) are standard batched
tensor operations that run in parallel on GPUs, and drawings are processed
independently, so throughput scales with batch size in batch-processing
environments. Given the length limits, we kept this to the response.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
R3.13 (complexity of transformer blocks vs. lighter alternatives)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The new ablation (Table 2) compares the full model with lighter variants and
reports their parameter counts: without the 56x56 branch (35.1 M), without the
28x28 attention (24.4 M) and a plain U-Net (10.2 M). The plain U-Net is clearly
worse on every metric, and removing the 56x56 branch lowers gap closure. The
bottleneck attention (19 M parameters) slightly improves fill F1 and bucket
accuracy but not gap closure, and the text now reports this openly.
